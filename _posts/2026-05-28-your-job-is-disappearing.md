  <!-- INTRO -->
  <div class="card prose">
    <p>From the mid-20th century until well past the dawn of the internet, a massive portion of project management was administrative. Armies of project coordinators sat in windowless rooms, manually updating physical Gantt charts with colored tape, cold-calling engineers for status updates, and compiling massive paper binders for monthly steering committee reviews.</p>
    <p>Then, desktop scheduling software arrived, followed by cloud-based issue trackers. The physical boards were wiped out. It's the classic story of technology erasing a job — and it's the story everyone's afraid of with AI.</p>
    <p>If your job is defined as a collection of administrative tasks, you are right to be nervous. But if your job is solving operational and delivery complexity, the reality is entirely different. <strong>The job of an Engineering Project Manager (EPM) or Technical Program Manager (TPM) was never a single task.</strong> We get paid to align human intent, manage risk, and ship complex systems. The specific mechanics keep changing underneath us.</p>
    <p style="margin-bottom:0">Here is how the project management layer has evolved — and where it is going next.</p>
  </div>

  <!-- STAGE 1 -->
  <div class="stage-section">
    <div class="stage-visual">
      <div class="stage-icon" style="background:linear-gradient(135deg,#ede9fe,#c4b5fd)">📋</div>
      <div class="stage-num-badge" style="background:#ede9fe;color:#7c3aed">Stage 1</div>
      <div class="stage-era-label">Early Software<br>Era</div>
    </div>
    <div class="stage-body">
      <div class="stage-title">Tracking Status by Hand</div>
      <div class="was-now">
        <span class="wn-pill wn-was">Was: human router for data</span>
        <span class="wn-arrow">→</span>
        <span class="wn-pill wn-now">Now: automated workflows</span>
      </div>
      <p class="stage-prose">Early in the software era, project management was fiercely manual. You chased engineers down to get status updates. You manually copied data from a spreadsheet into a PowerPoint deck for leadership. If a project had fifty moving parts, you had to manually ping fifty people. When a deadline was missed, you were the one updating individual date fields across a massive, brittle schedule.</p>
      <p class="stage-prose">Within a decade, those tasks evaporated. A developer closing a pull request automatically moved the ticket and updated the burn-down chart. Status tracking became a byproduct of actual engineering work rather than a separate bureaucratic tax.</p>
      <div class="shift-callout">The scale of modern cross-functional engineering outran the manual technique. The PM didn't disappear — they stopped being a human router.</div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <!-- STAGE 2 -->
  <div class="stage-section flip">
    <div class="stage-visual">
      <div class="stage-icon" style="background:linear-gradient(135deg,#d1fae5,#6ee7b7)">🗺️</div>
      <div class="stage-num-badge" style="background:#d1fae5;color:#059669">Stage 2</div>
      <div class="stage-era-label">Requirements<br>Era</div>
    </div>
    <div class="stage-body">
      <div class="stage-title">Defining Scope: The WBS & Requirements Gathering</div>
      <div class="was-now">
        <span class="wn-pill wn-was">Was: weeks of stakeholder meetings</span>
        <span class="wn-arrow">→</span>
        <span class="wn-pill wn-now">Now: AI-drafted in minutes</span>
      </div>
      <p class="stage-prose">There was a time when breaking a massive product vision down into a Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) or an Epic-and-Story hierarchy ate up weeks of administrative time. You sat in grueling meetings, pulling requirements out of stakeholders, trying to translate ambiguous product statements into clean, actionable technical milestones.</p>
      <p class="stage-prose">Today, you can feed a raw product requirements document into an agent framework and watch it output a comprehensive WBS, assign initial sizing, and draft technical tasks in minutes. The part that surprises people is how much of what we thought of as "project management skill" was just working around primitive tools.</p>
      <div class="shift-callout" style="border-color:#059669">Better tools eliminate the toll. The manager is freed to ask bigger questions: <em>Is this the right architecture? Are we solving for the right constraints?</em></div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <!-- STAGE 3 -->
  <div class="stage-section">
    <div class="stage-visual">
      <div class="stage-icon" style="background:linear-gradient(135deg,#fef3c7,#fcd34d)">⚙️</div>
      <div class="stage-num-badge" style="background:#fef3c7;color:#d97706">Stage 3</div>
      <div class="stage-era-label">SDLC /<br>CI/CD Era</div>
    </div>
    <div class="stage-body">
      <div class="stage-title">Managing Execution: Driving the SDLC</div>
      <div class="was-now">
        <span class="wn-pill wn-was">Was: gatekeeper of the release train</span>
        <span class="wn-arrow">→</span>
        <span class="wn-pill wn-now">Now: architect of the delivery process</span>
      </div>
      <p class="stage-prose">Guiding a project through the Software Development Life Cycle used to require heavy operational babysitting. Project managers coordinated code freezes, ran triage meetings to manually deduplicate bugs, and signed off on deployment checklists.</p>
      <p class="stage-prose">Now, the SDLC is shifting from human-authored tracking to agent-observed governance. CI/CD pipelines, automated testing, and intelligent error triaging handle the execution mechanics.</p>
      <div class="shift-callout" style="border-color:#d97706">Compliance, security, and velocity are now built into the platform engineering lifecycle — not checked manually at the end.</div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <!-- STAGE 4 -->
  <div class="stage-section flip">
    <div class="stage-visual">
      <div class="stage-icon" style="background:linear-gradient(135deg,#fee2e2,#fca5a5)">🕸️</div>
      <div class="stage-num-badge" style="background:#fee2e2;color:#dc2626">Stage 4</div>
      <div class="stage-era-label">Microservices<br>Era</div>
    </div>
    <div class="stage-body">
      <div class="stage-title">Mitigating Risk: The Distributed Dependency Problem</div>
      <div class="was-now">
        <span class="wn-pill wn-was">Was: "When will the API be ready?"</span>
        <span class="wn-arrow">→</span>
        <span class="wn-pill wn-now">Now: governing the ecosystem</span>
      </div>
      <p class="stage-prose">In the era of massive monolithic systems moving to distributed microservices, the hardest-won knowledge for an EPM was managing cross-team dependencies. If Team A needed an API from Team B, you hand-rolled the alignment logic — recurring syncs, risk registers, negotiated timelines between siloed engineering orgs.</p>
      <p class="stage-prose">One by one, platforms solved the plumbing. Shared data platforms, atomic APIs, and service meshes made it easier for systems to decouple. But when the cost of spinning up a new service drops to near zero, the volume of services explodes.</p>
      <div class="shift-callout" style="border-color:#dc2626">The frontier moves from tracking dates to managing systemic architectural health — data lineage, translation workflows, global ecosystem governance.</div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <!-- STAGE 5 -->
  <div class="stage-section">
    <div class="stage-visual">
      <div class="stage-icon" style="background:linear-gradient(135deg,#dbeafe,#93c5fd)">📊</div>
      <div class="stage-num-badge" style="background:#dbeafe;color:#1d4ed8">Stage 5</div>
      <div class="stage-era-label">Analytics<br>Era</div>
    </div>
    <div class="stage-body">
      <div class="stage-title">Stakeholder Alignment: The Data Layer of Leadership</div>
      <div class="was-now">
        <span class="wn-pill wn-was">Was: building the chart manually</span>
        <span class="wn-arrow">→</span>
        <span class="wn-pill wn-now">Now: interpreting it strategically</span>
      </div>
      <p class="stage-prose">The most bureaucratic work a project manager used to do was defending the project's health to leadership committees. You read through metrics by hand to explain a slip in velocity. You sat through alignment sessions begging for resource allocations from executive priesthoods who guarded headcount.</p>
      <p class="stage-prose">That ground has shifted. Automated analytics dashboards mean data is democratized. Leadership can see real-time health metrics, financial burns, and predictive completion lines without needing a PM to manually build the chart.</p>
      <div class="shift-callout" style="border-color:#1d4ed8">Because the data is transparent, the EPM's role shifts from reporting it to interpreting it. You own the narrative, the edge cases, and the strategy when things go wrong at scale.</div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <!-- STAGE 6 -->
  <div class="stage-section flip">
    <div class="stage-visual">
      <div class="stage-icon" style="background:linear-gradient(135deg,#fef9c3,#fde047)">🤖</div>
      <div class="stage-num-badge" style="background:#fef9c3;color:#a16207">Stage 6</div>
      <div class="stage-era-label">AI Era —<br>Now</div>
    </div>
    <div class="stage-body">
      <div class="stage-title">Agentic Delivery: The Current Disappearance</div>
      <div class="was-now">
        <span class="wn-pill wn-was">Was: mechanical PM tasks</span>
        <span class="wn-arrow">→</span>
        <span class="wn-pill wn-now">Now: execution judgment</span>
      </div>
      <p class="stage-prose">AI can now generate project plans, track dependencies, write status reports from Slack history, and synthesize risk from code commits. When you see this, you can't help but think the thing you're doing is going away. <strong>If your job is defined as a collection of administrative tasks — you are right to be nervous.</strong></p>
      <p class="stage-prose">But if your job is solving operational and delivery complexity, the reality is entirely different. No AI tool has automated execution judgment, cross-functional empathy, or strategic negotiation. As long as engineering organizations are solving novel, high-stakes problems under real-world constraints, the supply of complexity will never run out.</p>
      <div class="shift-callout" style="border-color:#a16207">Tasks run out. Project plans can be auto-generated. Complexity never runs out.</div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <!-- CONCLUSION -->
  <div class="conclusion">
    <div style="font-size:36px;margin-bottom:16px">⚡</div>
    <h2 style="font-size:1.5rem;font-weight:800;color:#111827;margin-bottom:12px">The Premium on Judgment Only Goes Up</h2>
    <p style="font-size:15px;color:#4b5563;max-width:600px;margin:0 auto;line-height:1.75">Every time a manual PM task gets automated away, we face the same nervous question: <em>What will I do with my time now?</em> The answer is always the same — more impactful work, harder than before. The machine takes the mechanical parts with a known answer and leaves us the parts that require deep human judgment.</p>
    <div class="three-col">
      <div class="truth-card">
        <div class="truth-label">What gets automated</div>
        <div class="truth-text">Scheduling, ticket routing, status synthesis — anything with a known answer.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="truth-card">
        <div class="truth-label">What stays human</div>
        <div class="truth-text">Execution judgment, cross-functional empathy, strategic negotiation under real constraints.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="truth-card">
        <div class="truth-label">What this means for you</div>
        <div class="truth-text">Dive deep into technical architecture. Understanding is the raw material of judgment.</div>
